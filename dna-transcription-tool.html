How it works

      
The method behind the numbers

    

    

      
What the tool does
It reads your DNA as the coding strand (or as the template strand if you choose it), writes the mRNA by replacing T with U, and translates it with the standard genetic code in all six reading frames, three on each strand. The longest open reading frame is shown separately.

      
How melting temperature is estimated
For sequences shorter than 14 bases it uses the Wallace rule, Tm = 2(A+T) + 4(G+C). For longer sequences it uses the basic formula Tm = 64.9 + 41 × (G+C − 16.4) / N, where N is the length. Both are rough guides; salt and primer concentration change the real value.

      
Worked example
ATGGCC transcribes to AUGGCC and translates in frame 1 to Met-Ala (MA). With 4 of 6 bases G or C, the GC content is 66.7%.

Questions

      

        
Is this DNA transcription tool free?

        
Yes. It is free, needs no sign-up, and runs entirely in your browser — your sequence is never uploaded or stored.

        
What is the difference between the coding and template options?

        
The mRNA sequence matches the coding strand (with U instead of T). If you paste the template strand instead, choose the template option and the tool works from its reverse complement.

        
Which genetic code and conventions does it use?

        
The standard genetic code. Stop codons are shown as *, codons containing ambiguous bases as X, and the longest ORF is the longest ATG-to-stop stretch across all six frames.
